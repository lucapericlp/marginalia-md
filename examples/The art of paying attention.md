Reading changes when we leave ourselves enough room to answer. A margin can hold an objection, a connection, or the beginning of an idea that belongs to the reader.[^mn-p-conversation]

A highlight says that something mattered. A note asks why. In that small pause, ==a question becomes a way of paying attention==[^mn-s-attention]. We move from collecting sentences to examining what they ask us to believe.

## Keep the question open

A convincing explanation can make its assumptions disappear. Try setting the conclusion aside for a moment: what evidence would change your mind, and what would a careful critic notice first?[^mn-p-assumptions]

Not every thought needs to be resolved while reading. Some ideas become useful only after they have met another book, a conversation, or a difficult experience. Leave a little room for that encounter.

## Return to the source

A good annotation keeps the original words within reach. The point is to ==make a thought easier to revisit==[^mn-s-return], with enough context to understand why it caught your attention the first time.

Close the note and come back tomorrow. The words will be the same; the reader may have changed.
